Every manhole in Kentucky goes somewhere.

Vanilla paints 446 manhole covers on its streets and puts nothing under them. Sewars digs the sewers: brick and concrete tunnels running under the streets of every town, laid out from the game's own street map, so a highway is a trunk sewer with a sludge channel down it, a street is a main, and a back lane is a culvert you can barely turn round in.

- Right-click a manhole cover in the road: Climb down into the sewer. A crowbar or a pipe wrench makes quicker work of the lid.
- Every cover has a ladder under it. Right-click a ladder: Climb out to the street, and you come up through the cover above it.
- It is dark. Daylight comes down through the holes in the covers; bring a torch for the rest.
- The dead are down here too.
- Shelters: other survivors dug in before you. Maintenance rooms, pump stations, squats and last stands behind steel doors, some stocked, some still occupied.
- Build down there. The tunnels are yours to wall off, barricade and furnish; the mod never touches anything you build.
- 16 towns, 442 shafts, 93 shelters, over 110,000 squares of tunnel.

THE SEWER MAP
The sewers are something you learn. Your map starts black and fills in as you walk: the ladders you have used (with the street above), the shelters you have found. Open it with N (rebindable in Options -> Mods) or Sewer map on the right-click menu below ground. Municipal sewer plans, left in maintenance rooms and pump stations, reveal whole sheets of a town at once, and the journals the shelters' builders left behind point you to the next place worth finding.

The tunnels sit one level under the real streets, so they are exactly where they should be: walk under Main Street and you come up on Main Street.

Single player, hosted co-op and dedicated servers. Build 42.

Sandbox: how many of the dead are down there.
