Every manhole in Kentucky goes somewhere.

Vanilla paints 446 manhole covers on its streets and puts nothing under them. This mod digs the sewers: brick and concrete tunnels running under the streets of every town, laid out from the game's own street map, so a highway is a trunk sewer with a sludge channel down it, a street is a main, and a back lane is a culvert you can barely turn round in.

- Right-click a manhole cover in the road: Climb down into the sewer. A crowbar or a pipe wrench makes quicker work of the lid.
- Every cover has a ladder under it. Right-click a ladder: Climb out to the street, and you come up through the cover above it.
- Louisville too. Vanilla gives all of Louisville 13 covers, and none to Irvington, Brandenburg, March Ridge or Valley Station. The mod adds its own there, at the junctions and along the long streets, with sewers under every street.
- It is dark. Daylight comes down through the holes in the covers; bring a torch for the rest.
- The dead are down here too. So are the rats.
- Shelters: other survivors dug in before you. Maintenance rooms, pump stations, squats and last stands behind steel doors, some stocked, some still occupied.
- Broken walls and dug caves: somebody knocked through the tunnel wall and kept digging. A winding dirt passage, and a hideout at the end of it.
- Houses with a way down: a trapdoor in the floor of some garages, kitchens and storerooms, and a ladder down to the sewer under the street.
- Somewhere in the world there is a rat hole where you can take the bricks apart and enter to find many aggressive ROUS's and a hidden cache.
- It's possible there are hidden cult members who worship the ROUS's somewhere else in the map
- Annotated maps to both turn up where maps turn up: in a drawer, in a glovebox, on the dead.
- Locked gates: half the county's maintenance rooms and pump stations sit behind barred grilles. Each town has one Sewer Maintenance Key, in the rooms that are not locked and on some of the dead in sanitation overalls. Keep it in your hands or on a key ring. A grille latches behind you once the room is empty, and never shuts anybody in.
- Sewer gas: some narrow culverts, well away from the ladders, hold foul air behind the county's warning placards. Without a gas mask or respirator it makes you sick, and it costs health if you stay; with one, it uses up your filter.
- Storm drains at the kerb: 378 of the game's own kerb grates are ways down too. A tight squeeze: not for the overweight or the overloaded (a sandbox setting says who fits).
- Storm-drain outfalls: down on the banks of the river, the creeks and the lakes, an iron grate in the ground. A way into the sewers nobody on the street sees, and a way out of town the dead will not be waiting at.
- Build down there. The tunnels are yours to wall off, barricade and furnish; the mod never touches anything you build.
- Dig. With a pickaxe (or, slowly, a sledgehammer), right-click below ground and Dig: the rock beside you becomes one more square of floor, and a wall with a space behind it comes down. A pipe bomb set off in a tunnel opens the rock for two squares round it. Doors, a ladder's wall and anybody's basement never give.
- Out of the wind. No wind chill below ground: the day's own cold and no more. Nothing down there warms you.
- Out of earshot. The dead on the street do not come to the noise you make under it, and the dead in the tunnels do not come to the noise you make on the street; the ones on your own level do. Climb down a manhole and whoever was chasing you gives up.
- 31 towns, 1,174 manholes that open, 378 kerb storm drains, 16 storm-drain outfalls, 239 shelters (47 behind locked gates), 152 caves, 189 houses with a way down, 97 stretches of sewer gas, over 480,000 squares of tunnel.

THE SEWER MAP
The sewers are something you learn. Your map starts black and fills in as you walk: the ladders you have used (with the street above), the shelters you have found. Open it with Sewer map on the right-click menu below ground. Municipal sewer plans, left in maintenance rooms and pump stations, reveal whole sheets of a town at once, and the journals the shelters' builders left behind point you to the next place worth finding.

The tunnels sit one level under the real streets, so they are exactly where they should be: walk under Main Street and you come up on Main Street.

Single player, hosted co-op and dedicated servers. Build 42. In 27 languages.

ADDING IT TO A SAVE OR SERVER MID GAME
Yes, it works. Nothing is built ahead of time: the sewers are built a stretch at a time as players come near, so an existing world simply fills in as people explore it. The manholes are the map's own; the extra covers, house hatches and everything below go in the first time someone comes near, and anything already underground that is not the mod's is left alone. On a server, add the mod and its Workshop ID to the config and restart; every player needs it. Removing the mod from a save is the risky direction, so be up on the street when you do.

Sandbox: how many of the dead are down there, who they were (ordinary people or survivors with full packs), how many rats, how well the shelters are stocked, how bad the sewer gas is (off, mild, harmful or deadly), whether the sewers can be dug (off, picks and hammers, or explosives too), whether the street hears the sewer (no, or yes: the game's own rule), and how many EXIT stencils are on the walls (none, few, or by every ladder). Each player's own, under Options -> Mods -> Sewers: Sounds in the pipes, off unless ticked. More for servers below.

New in 0.9: THE LABYRINTH, 26 MORE LANGUAGES, AND SETTINGS FOR SERVERS.

The labyrinth. A trapdoor under the weeds in the deep woods west of Muldraugh, and under it a brick maze 256 squares a side with no light and one way through. The dead who went in before you are still there. Torches, water and tins in its dead ends, rooms to rest in, a hoard at its heart, and a way out through a brick hut that is no way in. Your map draws only what you have walked. It cannot be dug through.

Languages. The mod now speaks every language the game does: Spanish (Spain, Argentina, Chile, Mexico), Portuguese (Portugal, Brazil), French, German, Italian, Dutch, Catalan, Russian, Ukrainian, Polish, Czech, Hungarian, Romanian, Danish, Norwegian, Finnish, Turkish, Indonesian, Thai, Korean, Japanese and Chinese (Simplified and Traditional). You see your own game language; nothing to set.

For roleplay servers, and anybody who wants more say. Admins (moderators and game masters too) get their own right-click menu, Sewers (admin). With it: decide who may use any manhole cover, trapdoor or grate (both ways, in only, out only, or nobody); put a new way down on any square of street, with a passage dug to the nearest tunnel; draw your own tunnels on the sewer map, 1, 3 or 5 wide, joined to the mod's or kept apart; go down two more levels (2 and 3) by shafts, each with its own map; set down copies of the mod's own rooms, furnished, stocked and occupied; build a room of your own, keep it with a kind of stock (medical, military, guns, police, survival, food, tools, car parts, electronics, laboratory) and set copies of it down; mark a hand-built basement or bunker as indoors, so the game treats it as a real room; see the whole map; rebuild a damaged stretch. Sandbox settings too: every way down closed until an admin opens it, trapdoors in houses (yes or no), outfalls (yes or no), the rats' nest (open, or sealed until you say), how tough the rodents of unusual size are, how many dead are in the temple and in the labyrinth, the labyrinth itself (yes or no), how far a new way down may be from a tunnel, and where the labyrinth is, if another mod is in its ground. Server hosts: SERVERS.md in the mod's own folder is the full guide.
