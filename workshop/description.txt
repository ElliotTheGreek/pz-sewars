Every manhole in Kentucky goes somewhere.

Vanilla paints 446 manhole covers on its streets and puts nothing under them. Sewars digs the sewers: brick and concrete tunnels running under the streets of every town, laid out from the game's own street map, so a highway is a trunk sewer with a sludge channel down it, a street is a main, and a back lane is a culvert you can barely turn round in.

- Right-click a manhole cover in the road: Climb down into the sewer. A crowbar or a pipe wrench makes quicker work of the lid.
- Every cover has a ladder under it. Right-click a ladder: Climb out to the street, and you come up through the cover above it.
- Louisville too. Vanilla gives all of Louisville 13 covers, and none to Irvington, Brandenburg, March Ridge or Valley Station. Sewars adds its own there, at the junctions and along the long streets, with sewers under every street.
- It is dark. Daylight comes down through the holes in the covers; bring a torch for the rest.
- The dead are down here too. So are the rats.
- Shelters: other survivors dug in before you. Maintenance rooms, pump stations, squats and last stands behind steel doors, some stocked, some still occupied.
- Broken walls and dug caves: somebody knocked through the tunnel wall and kept digging. A winding dirt passage, and a hideout at the end of it.
- Houses with a way down: a trapdoor in the floor of some garages, kitchens and storerooms, and a ladder down to the sewer under the street.
- Somewhere in the world there is a rat hole where you can take the bricks apart and enter to find many aggressive ROUS's and a hidden cache.
- It's possible there are hidden cult members who worship the ROUS's somewhere else in the map
- Annotated maps to both turn up where maps turn up: in a drawer, in a glovebox, on the dead.
- Locked gates: half the county's maintenance rooms and pump stations sit behind barred grilles. Each town has one Sewer Maintenance Key, in the rooms that are not locked and on some of the dead in sanitation overalls. Keep it in your hands or on a key ring. A grille latches behind you once the room is empty, and never shuts anybody in.
- Sewer gas: some narrow culverts, well away from the ladders, hold foul air behind the county's warning placards. Without a gas mask or respirator it makes you sick, and it costs health if you stay; with one, it uses up your filter.
- Storm-drain outfalls: down on the banks of the river, the creeks and the lakes, an iron grate in the ground. A way into the sewers nobody on the street sees, and a way out of town the dead will not be waiting at.
- Build down there. The tunnels are yours to wall off, barricade and furnish; the mod never touches anything you build.
- Dig. With a pickaxe (or, slowly, a sledgehammer), right-click below ground and Dig: the rock beside you becomes one more square of floor, and a wall with a space behind it comes down. A pipe bomb set off in a tunnel opens the rock for two squares round it. Doors, a ladder's wall and anybody's basement never give.
- Out of earshot. The dead on the street do not come to the noise you make under it; the ones in the tunnels with you do.
- 31 towns, 1,174 manholes that open, 16 storm-drain outfalls, 239 shelters (47 behind locked gates), 152 caves, 189 houses with a way down, 97 stretches of sewer gas, over 479,000 squares of tunnel.

THE SEWER MAP
The sewers are something you learn. Your map starts black and fills in as you walk: the ladders you have used (with the street above), the shelters you have found. Open it with K (rebindable in Options -> Mods) or Sewer map on the right-click menu below ground. Municipal sewer plans, left in maintenance rooms and pump stations, reveal whole sheets of a town at once, and the journals the shelters' builders left behind point you to the next place worth finding.

The tunnels sit one level under the real streets, so they are exactly where they should be: walk under Main Street and you come up on Main Street.

Single player, hosted co-op and dedicated servers. Build 42.

ADDING IT TO A SAVE OR SERVER MID GAME
Yes, it works. Nothing is built ahead of time: the sewers are built a stretch at a time as players come near, so an existing world simply fills in as people explore it. The manholes are the map's own; the extra covers, house hatches and everything below go in the first time someone comes near, and anything already underground that is not the mod's is left alone. On a server, add the mod and its Workshop ID to the config and restart; every player needs it. A save that never had the mod uses the default sandbox settings for it. In a house someone already lives in, a trapdoor can turn up under their furniture: it is only a picture on the floor. Removing the mod from a save is the risky direction, so be up on the street when you do.

Sandbox: how many of the dead are down there, who they were (ordinary people or survivors with full packs), how many rats, how well the shelters are stocked, how bad the sewer gas is (off, mild, harmful or deadly), whether the sewers can be dug (off, picks and hammers, or explosives too), and whether the street hears the sewer (no, or yes: the game's own rule).
